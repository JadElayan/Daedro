How teams use it

From an incident to a proven fix

A pick fails, a shift goes badly, a new gripper behaves differently. Daedro keeps that evidence and turns it into a better robot, instead of losing it in logs and chat threads.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Import the recording your cell already keeps. Daedro finds the moment that matters.

  2. 02

    Make it a case

    The clip, robot state, gripper, software version and part, kept together.

  3. 03

    Reproduce

    Check whether simulation recreates the incident, at the recorded conditions and nearby.

  4. 04

    Try a fix

    Run the current and proposed behaviour against the case: what it resolves, what it doesn't, what it breaks.

  5. 05

    Verify everywhere

    Get a verdict for every cell and hardware setup before rollout, including where only a hardware test will do.

  6. 06

    Keep what you learned

    Log hardware trials and real outcomes. Every case becomes a test that guards future changes.

Prototype scope

Works now

  • Cases from MCAP recordings with JSON channels (the format Daedro and Foxglove's JSON tools write): incident detection, clips, timelines
  • Reproduce incidents and test fixes in MuJoCo; Franka Panda trusted, SO-101 experimental
  • Release records per hardware setup, failure library, pull-request checks, video replays
  • Your own Python controller, including camera observations for vision policies
  • Logging hardware trials and post-rollout outcomes against each case

Not yet

  • Reading ROS 2 messages (CDR) directly from your bags
  • Building simulation from your own robot and cell models
  • Hosted runs, team accounts and dashboards
  • Automatic outcome tracking from your fleet, or any hardware validation

A recording shows what happened, not what a changed robot would have done. Passing in simulation does not guarantee real-world safety.
